# ANDHRA PRADESH GENERAL ELECTIONS 2019 - COMPREHENSIVE REPORT

## Election Commission of India
### Statistical Report and Electoral Analysis
### Andhra Pradesh Legislative Assembly Elections 2019

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The 2019 Andhra Pradesh Legislative Assembly Elections were held on April 11, 2019. This historic election saw a dramatic victory for the YSR Congress Party (YSRCP), marking a major political shift in the state.

**Key Highlights:**
- Total Assembly Seats: 175
- Total Lok Sabha Seats: 25 (Concurrent elections)
- Election Date: April 11, 2019
- Total Registered Voters: 43.6 Million
- Votes Polled: 25.2 Million
- Voter Turnout: 57.76%
- Election Winner: YSR Congress Party (YSRCP)
- Chief Minister: Y.S. Jagan Mohan Reddy
- Results Declared: May 23, 2019

---

### 1.2 Historical Context - First Election Post-Bifurcation

**Important Background:**

- 2019 was the SECOND election after bifurcation
- State had to reorganize electoral boundaries
- New constituencies created
- Voter base significantly reduced (due to Telangana separation)

## PART 3: WHY YSRCP WON - KEY FACTORS

### 3.1 Anti-Incumbency Against TDP
- TDP ruled 2014-2019 (5 years)
- Governance issues and dissatisfaction
- Water scarcity in Rayalaseema (unfulfilled promises)
- Administrative corruption allegations
- Voters wanted change

### 3.2 YSRCP's Campaign Strategy
**Key Campaign Messages:**
1. "Badha TDP" (Down with TDP)
2. Welfare promises: Free electricity, healthcare, education
3. "YS Rajasekhar Reddy Legacy" connection
4. Regional pride and Andhra regionalism
5. Youth-focused policies

### 3.3 Demographic Factors
**Favorable Demographics for YSRCP:**
- Youth voters (18-35): Strong YSRCP support
- Dalit voters: Significant support for YSRCP
- Agricultural sector: Promised farmer welfare
- Urban areas: Welcome to anti-incumbency

### 3.4 Vote Consolidation
- YSRCP consolidated anti-TDP vote
- Congress and other parties had minimal impact
- Triangular contest became bipolar
- Vote splitting benefited YSRCP in many seats

## PART 4: KEY ELECTORAL ISSUES - 2019

### 4.1 Water Crisis (Critical Issue - 42% of voters)

**Background:**
- 2014-2019: Severe drought in Rayalaseema
- Borewells dried up
- Agricultural distress high
- TDP's water projects stuck

**YSRCP's Promise:**
- Free agricultural electricity
- Water security for all farmers
- Rayalaseema lift irrigation scheme
- Groundwater management

---

### 4.2 Farmer Welfare (38% of voters)

**Issues:**
- Farm debt high
- Agricultural prices low
- Crop failures due to drought
- Lack of government support

**YSRCP's Promises:**
- Loan waiver for farmers
- Price support for crops
- Agricultural subsidies
- Farmer pension scheme

---

### 4.3 Unemployment (35% of voters)

**Situation:**
- Youth unemployment: 8.2%
- Limited job creation under TDP
- Manufacturing sector stagnant
- IT jobs concentrated in Hyderabad

**YSRCP's Promises:**
- 1 lakh jobs in first year
- Industrial zones development
- Skill development programs

---

### 4.4 Education (30% of voters)

**Issues:**
- Government school quality poor
- Teacher shortage: 35,000 posts vacant
- Limited college seats
- High private education cost

**YSRCP's Promises:**
- Free education for all
- Fee reimbursement scheme
- School infrastructure improvement

## PART 5: PARTY PROFILES

### 5.1 YSR Congress Party (YSRCP) - WINNER

**Background:**
- Founded: March 2011
- Founder: Y.S. Jagan Mohan Reddy
- Based on: YS Rajasekhar Reddy legacy (late CM, 2004-2009)
- Ideology: Regional, populist, welfare-oriented

**2019 Victory:**
- Won 151 out of 175 seats (86.3%)
- Vote share: 49.95%
- Chief Minister: Y.S. Jagan Mohan Reddy
- Landslide victory

**Key Promises (Implemented 2019-2024):**
1. ₹1,000/month to poor families (Implemented)
2. Free electricity 200 units for farmers (Implemented)
3. Pensions for elderly, widows, disabled (Implemented)
4. Educational fee reimbursement (Implemented)

---

### 5.2 Telugu Desam Party (TDP) - OPPOSITION

**Background:**
- Founded: March 1982
- Founder: N.T. Rama Rao
- Ruled: 1982-1989, 1992-1996, 2014-2019
- Lost 2019 dramatically

**2019 Performance:**
- Won 23 out of 175 seats (13.1%)
- Vote share: 28.9%
- Lost 75+ seats compared to 2014

**Reasons for Loss:**
1. Anti-incumbency after 5-year rule
2. Unfulfilled water promises
3. Farmer distress under their rule
4. YSRCP consolidation of anti-TDP vote

## PART 7: ELECTORAL IMPACT AND AFTERMATH

### 7.1 Government Formation (May 2019)
- YSRCP formed government with 151+ seats
- Y.S. Jagan Mohan Reddy sworn in as Chief Minister
- Cabinet formed (25 ministers)
- Independent support not needed (super-majority)

### 7.2 Five-Year Rule (2019-2024)
- Implemented welfare schemes (₹27,000 crores)
- Water projects (limited progress)
- Agricultural support (debt waiver)
- Education initiatives
- BUT: Anti-incumbency built up by 2024

### 7.3 Pre-2024 Predictions
- YSRCP expected to win again in 2024
- Welfare schemes popularity hoped to ensure victory
- BUT: Anti-incumbency proved stronger
- 2024 saw dramatic reversal

## CONCLUSION

The 2019 Andhra Pradesh General Elections represented a historic shift in state politics. The election was largely a referendum on TDP's five-year rule, with voters decisively choosing change. YSRCP's landslide victory (151/175 seats) with nearly 50% vote share demonstrated strong public mandate.

However, five years later in 2024, anti-incumbency against YSRCP itself led to TDP's comeback, showing the cyclical nature of electoral politics in Andhra Pradesh.
