# ANDHRA PRADESH GENERAL ELECTIONS 2004 - COMPREHENSIVE REPORT

## Election Commission of India
### Statistical Report and Electoral Analysis
### Andhra Pradesh Legislative Assembly Elections 2004 (Pre-Welfare Era)

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The 2004 Andhra Pradesh Legislative Assembly Elections were held on November 3, 2004 and November 10, 2004 (two phases). This election brought Y.S. Rajasekhar Reddy to power and initiated the "welfare era" in AP politics.

**Key Highlights:**
- Total Assembly Seats: 294 (Undivided AP)
- Total Lok Sabha Seats: 42
- Election Phases: Two phases (November 2004)
- Total Registered Voters: 66.8 Million
- Votes Polled: 37.2 Million
- Voter Turnout: 55.7%
- Election Winner: Indian National Congress
- Chief Minister: Y.S. Rajasekhar Reddy
- Results Declared: November 12, 2004

## PART 2: POLITICAL CONTEXT - 2004

### 2.1 Pre-2004 Government

**Previous Chief Minister:**
- Name: N. Chandrababu Naidu (TDP)
- Tenure: 1995-2004 (9 years)
- Known for: IT sector development, "PC-style" governance
- Performance: Mixed - pro-industry but high-handed governance
- Anti-incumbency: Strong anti-TDP sentiment

---

### 2.2 Why TDP Lost in 2004

**Major Reasons:**
1. **Anti-Incumbency:** 9 years in power (1995-2004)
2. **Farmer Distress:** High agricultural distress, farmer suicides
3. **Poor Governance:** Authoritarian style, corruption
4. **Rural Anger:** Development focused on cities, not rural areas
5. **Land Acquisition:** Forced land acquisition for projects
6. **High-Handed Politics:** NTR's "Hyderabadi" (outsider) image
7. **National Context:** Congress's Rajiv Gandhi assassination (2004) sympathy wave helped INC

---

### 2.3 Y.S. Rajasekhar Reddy - Emerging Leader

**Background:**
- Name: Yeddysetty Sudhakar Reddy
- Known as: Y.S. Rajasekhar Reddy
- Region: Rayalaseema (Kadapa district)
- Background: Wealthy, pro-farmer image
- Political Career: Switched from TDP to Congress
- Strategy: Anti-TDP, pro-farmer, welfare focus

**Campaign Theme (2004):**
- "Farmers are in distress"
- "Need welfare, not development for rich"
- "Free electricity for farmers"
- "Education for poor children"
- "Healthcare for masses"

## PART 4: KEY ELECTORAL ISSUES - 2004

### 4.1 Farmer Distress (Critical - 50% of voters)

**Background:**
- Agricultural crisis in AP
- Farmer suicides (2000-2004): Thousands
- Chilli prices crashed
- Cotton prices low
- Groundnut crisis

**Congress Campaign:**
- Focus on farmer welfare
- Free electricity for farmers (promised)
- Crop insurance
- Loan assistance
- "Farmer-friendly government"

**TDP's Record:**
- Focused on IT and industry
- Rural areas neglected
- High-handed land acquisition
- Farmers faced exploitation
- Strong anti-farmer sentiment

---

### 4.2 Water Scarcity (35% of voters)

**Issues:**
- Krishna River water shortage
- Godavari River disputes with other states
- Rayalaseema severe drought (2000-2004)
- Irrigation projects incomplete

**Congress Promise:**
- Accelerate irrigation projects
- Resolve river water disputes
- Groundwater management

---

### 4.3 Education (30% of voters)

**TDP's Policy:**
- privatization of education
- High school fees
- Poor government schools

**Congress Promise:**
- Free education for poor
- Scholarships
- Improve government schools

---

### 4.4 Healthcare (25% of voters)

**Issues:**
- Poor rural healthcare
- Expensive private hospitals
- Government hospitals inadequate

**Congress Promise:**
- Free healthcare for poor
- Government hospitals improvement

## PART 7: THE WELFARE POLITICS BEGINNING

### 7.1 Free Electricity for Farmers (Game Changer)

**Promise (2004):**
- Free electricity for agricultural use
- Implemented from 2005

**Reality:**
- Hugely popular scheme
- Cost: ₹6,000-8,000 crores/year
- Became political necessity
- Later adopted by other states (Punjab, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu)
- Changed rural electoral dynamics

**Impact:**
- Changed AP politics forever
- Made welfare central to elections
- Unsustainable fiscally but politically necessary
- Continues even after bifurcation

---

### 7.2 Education Scholarships

**Vidyalakshmi Scheme (2005):**
- Free education for poor children
- Scholarships for SC/ST students
- Educational fee reimbursement
- Highly popular among poor

---

### 7.3 Healthcare - Aarogyasri Scheme (2007):**
- Health insurance for poor
- Free treatment at private hospitals
- Coverage: Millions of poor families
- Model later copied nationally

---

## PART 8: IMPACT AND LEGACY

### 8.1 Shift in Electoral Politics

**From 1995-2004 (TDP Era):**
- Development-focused (IT, industry)
- Pro-business policies
- Urban-centric
- High-tech image
- Pro-big business

**From 2004 onwards (Welfare Era):**
- Welfare-focused (free electricity, education)
- Pro-poor policies
- Rural-centric
- Social schemes focus
- Populism over sustainability

**Political Impact:**
- All parties had to adopt welfare schemes
- Made elections about welfare promises
- Fiscal burden on state
- Unsustainable but politically necessary

---

### 8.2 Y.S. Rajasekhar Reddy's Legacy

**Impact:**
- Established welfare as election issue
- Made free electricity electoral norm
- Focus on farmer welfare
- Education and healthcare emphasis

**Assassination:**
- Died in helicopter crash: May 25, 2009
- During second term
- Successor issues
- Political vacuum
- Led to instability until 2014 bifurcation

## CONCLUSION

The 2004 Andhra Pradesh General Elections marked a watershed moment in the state's electoral history. The election saw a clear rejection of TDP's "development-focused" model and embrace of Congress's welfare-oriented approach under Y.S. Rajasekhar Reddy. The victory initiated a new era where welfare schemes became central to electoral politics, a trend that fundamentally changed how elections were contested in Andhra Pradesh.

The introduction of free electricity for farmers, education scholarships, and healthcare schemes set a precedent that subsequent governments had to follow, making welfare populism the dominant electoral currency in the state.
