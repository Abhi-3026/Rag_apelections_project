## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The 2024 Andhra Pradesh Legislative Assembly Elections were held on May 13, 2024. This report provides comprehensive data on election results, electoral performance, constituency-wise analysis, and demographic insights.

**Key Highlights:**
- Total Assembly Seats: 174 (State Assembly only)
- Total Lok Sabha Seats: 25 (Concurrent with Assembly Elections)
- Election Date: May 13, 2024
- Total Registered Voters: 51.2 Million
- Votes Polled: 33.6 Million
- Voter Turnout: 65.7%
- Election Winner: Telugu Desam Party (TDP) Alliance
- Results Declared: May 24, 2024

## PART 4: KEY ELECTORAL ISSUES - 2024

### 4.1 Water and Irrigation (Critical Issue - 45% of voters)

**Background:**
- Rayalaseema Region: Severe drought (2021-2023)
- Krishna River: Interstate water sharing disputes
- Godavari River: Pending allocations

**Election Campaign Issues:**
1. **Water Scarcity in Rayalaseema**
   - Districts affected: Anantapur, Kadapa, Kurnool
   - 4.2 Million people affected
   - Campaign promise: Additional water allocation
   - TDP Promise: 50,000 new borewells
   - YSRCP Record: Limited progress on water supply

2. **Irrigation Project Completion**
   - Pending projects: 12 major irrigation schemes
   - Cost: ₹8,500 crores
   - Expected completion: 2-3 years

3. **Groundwater Management**
   - Depletion rate: 2.5 meters/year in some areas
   - Monsoon dependency: 65%
   - Alternative sources: Needed urgently

### 4.2 Farmer Welfare and Agricultural Support (40% of voters)

**Current Situation:**
- Farmer Population: 4.5 Million farming families
- Average land holding: 1.2 hectares
- Agricultural income: ₹42,000/year (low)
- Debt: 65% farmers have agricultural debt

**Key Issues:**
1. **Crop Prices**
   - Chilli farmers: Facing 40% price reduction
   - Rice farmers: MSP not guaranteed at ₹2,100/quintal
   - Groundnut: Price volatility of ±30%
   - Campaign Issue: Price support guarantee

2. **Loan Waiver Scheme**
   - YSRCP implemented: ₹27,000 crores debt waiver (2019-2023)
   - Impact: Limited reach (60% of farmers benefited)
   - Issue: Loan waiver sustainability
   - TDP Promise: Continued and expanded waiver

3. **Fertilizer and Input Subsidies**
   - Current subsidy: 30% of cost
   - Farmer demand: 50% subsidy
   - Cost: ₹3,500 crores for state budget
   - Political Issue: High demand, limited funds

4. **Crop Insurance Scheme**
   - Coverage: Only 30% of farmers
   - Claims: 45% claims rejected due to documentation
   - Campaign Issue: Make scheme more accessible

### 4.3 Unemployment and Job Creation (38% of voters)

**Current Situation:**
- Total workforce: 15.2 Million
- Unemployment rate: 6.8%
- Educated unemployed: 2.1 Million
- Age group 18-30: Highest unemployment (9.2%)

**Key Issues:**
1. **Youth Unemployment**
   - Graduate unemployment: 8.5%
   - Engineering graduates: Limited jobs
   - Software jobs: Concentrated in Hyderabad (concentrated employment)
   
2. **Government Job Creation**
   - Vacancies in government: 18,000 positions
   - Application backlog: 500,000 candidates
   - Selection process: Ongoing
   - Issue: Slow recruitment

3. **Industrial Growth**
   - Manufacturing jobs: Stagnant
   - IT jobs: Limited to Hyderabad
   - Agricultural processing: Potential sector
   - Campaign Focus: Decentralized development

### 4.4 Education Quality and Accessibility (35% of voters)

**Current Status:**
- Schools: 42,500 government schools
- Students: 6.8 Million
- Pass rate: 72% (needs improvement)
- College seats: Limited (capacity issue)

**Key Issues:**
1. **School Infrastructure**
   - Schools without electricity: 12%
   - Schools without drinking water: 8%
   - Infrastructure investment: Inadequate

2. **Teacher Shortage**
   - Vacant teaching positions: 45,000
   - Student-teacher ratio: 1:35 (target 1:25)
   - Quality: Directly impacted by shortage

3. **College Education**
   - College seats: 450,000 (insufficient for 1.2M graduates)
   - Private college fees: ₹2-5 lakhs/year (unaffordable)
   - Government college quality: Needs improvement
   - Campaign Issue: Fee reduction, more seats

### 4.5 Healthcare Access (30% of voters)

**Current Situation:**
- Healthcare facilities: 850 government hospitals
- Population per hospital: 58,500 (high)
- Infant mortality rate: 35 per 1,000 live births
- Rural-urban divide: 3:1 in healthcare access

**Key Issues:**
1. **Rural Healthcare**
   - Primary health centers: Insufficient in remote areas
   - Doctor availability: 1 doctor per 10,000 in rural areas
   - Medicine stock: Often empty
   - Campaign: Need more rural clinics

2. **Maternal Mortality**
   - Maternal mortality ratio: 58 per 100,000
   - Target: Reduce to 40 (WHO standard)
   - Prevention: Need better prenatal care
   - Issue: Lack of awareness and facilities

3. **Cost of Healthcare**
   - Average hospital cost per stay: ₹15,000-50,000
   - Without insurance: 40% of people go into debt
   - Campaign Issue: Health insurance expansion

### 4.6 Caste-Based Reservations (25% of voters - Contentious)

**Current Status:**
- OBC Reservation: 54% (for candidates and jobs)
- SC Reservation: 15%
- ST Reservation: 6.5%
- General Category: 24.5%

**Election Issues:**
1. **OBC Sub-Categorization**
   - Demand: Divide 54% quota among various OBC categories
   - Opposition: Larger backward castes fear dilution
   - Political Stand: TDP supports, YSRCP opposes
   - Impact: Major voting issue in OBC-dominated areas

2. **General Category Competition**
   - Jobs available in general category: Very limited
   - General category unemployment: 5.2% (lower than others)
   - Perceived issue: General category thinks unfairly treated
   - Reality: OBC voters form larger electorate

## PART 7: ELECTORAL OBSERVATIONS AND ANALYSIS

### 7.1 Key Observations

**1. Consolidated Anti-Incumbency**
- YSRCP lost despite maintaining 30% vote share
- Reason: TDP+Congress+CPI consolidated the opposition
- Lesson: Vote consolidation matters more than raw vote share

**2. Farmer Vote Shift**
- TDP gained 15% more farmer votes than 2019
- Reason: Water and agricultural issues favored TDP's narrative
- Impact: Farmer's bloc voting was decisive in rural areas

**3. Caste Factor Remained Strong**
- OBC voting remained with TDP (65% support maintained)
- SC voting split more evenly (Congress gained due to alliance)
- ST voting: Limited alternative, majority with traditional parties

**4. Urban-Rural Divide**
- TDP: 65% vote share in rural areas, 55% in urban areas
- YSRCP: Remained competitive in urban areas (45%)
- Trend: Rural areas decisively favored TDP

**5. Gender Participation Gap Narrowing**
- Female participation increased from 62.6% to 63.8%
- Male participation increased from 65.7% to 67.2%
- Positive trend for democratic participation

### 7.2 Electoral Verdict Analysis

**Why TDP Won:**
1. **Anti-Incumbency Against YSRCP:** 5-year rule fatigue
2. **Opposition Consolidation:** Congress and CPI allied with YSRCP split anti-incumbency votes
3. **Agricultural Narrative:** TDP's water and farm-focused campaign resonated
4. **Organizational Strength:** TDP's 40-year organizational base
5. **Regional Pride:** TDP represents Andhra identity vs nationalist politics

**Why YSRCP Lost Despite 30% Votes:**
1. **Electoral Arithmetic:** Lost seats due to vote distribution
2. **Alliance Factor:** Opposition consolidated while YSRCP remained alone in many seats
3. **Governance Issues:** Welfare schemes couldn't overcome anti-incumbency
4. **Center-State Relations:** Central government's stance on AP affected campaign

## CONCLUSION

The 2024 Andhra Pradesh General Elections represent a significant shift in state politics. While vote share remained relatively stable, the electoral arithmetic favored the opposition consolidation. The result reflects strong anti-incumbency against the YSRCP and rural voters' emphasis on agricultural and water-related issues.

The TDP's return to power with a decisive mandate suggests voter preference for experienced administration and agrarian-focused policies. The election also highlights the continuing importance of regional identity politics in Andhra Pradesh.
