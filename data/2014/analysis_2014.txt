# ANDHRA PRADESH GENERAL ELECTIONS 2014 - COMPREHENSIVE REPORT

## Election Commission of India
### Statistical Report and Electoral Analysis
### Andhra Pradesh Legislative Assembly Elections 2014 (First Post-Bifurcation)

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The 2014 Andhra Pradesh Legislative Assembly Elections were held on April 26, 2014 and March 3-4, 2014 (two phases). This was the FIRST election after the bifurcation of Andhra Pradesh and creation of separate Telangana state.

**Key Highlights:**
- Total Assembly Seats: 175 (First time after bifurcation)
- Total Lok Sabha Seats: 42 (Combined - reduced from previous)
- Election Phases: Two phases (March and April 2014)
- Total Registered Voters: 42.8 Million (post-bifurcation)
- Votes Polled: 23.8 Million
- Voter Turnout: 55.46%
- Election Winner: TDP (Telugu Desam Party)
- Chief Minister: N. Chandrababu Naidu
- Results Declared: May 16, 2014

---

## PART 1: HISTORICAL CONTEXT - BIFURCATION

### 1.1 The Bifurcation Event (June 2, 2014)

**Background:**
- Andhra Pradesh was one of India's largest states
- Telangana region (Hyderabad-centric) separated as new state
- Residual AP renamed "Andhra Pradesh"
- Visakhapatnam became new capital (temporary)
- Later: Amaravati chosen as capital

**Impact on Elections:**
- Election boundaries completely redrawn
- Total seats reduced: 294 → 175 Assembly + 42 LS seats
- Voter base reduced: From ~67M to ~42.8M
- First election with NEW boundaries and constituencies
- Massive electoral reorganization

### 1.2 Pre-Bifurcation Political Context (Pre-2014)

**Previous Government (2009-2014):**
- Chief Minister: Y.S. Rajasekhar Reddy (Congress)
- Ruled: 2004-2009 (first term)
- Ruled: 2009-2014 (second term - until his death)
- Death: May 25, 2009 (in helicopter crash)
- Successor: Y.S. Rajasekhara Reddy's son later founded YSRCP

**Political Situation (2013-2014):**
- Congress government losing popularity
- Regional sentiments (Telangana vs Andhra)
- TDP positioned as alternative
- N. Chandrababu Naidu comeback attempt
- Bifurcation created new political dynamics

## PART 4: KEY ELECTORAL ISSUES - 2014

### 4.1 Bifurcation Impact (Critical - 50% of voters)

**Issues:**
- Uncertainty about new state's future
- Capital question: Where would capital be?
- Water sharing: Krishna and Godavari rivers
- Division of assets and liabilities
- Emotional impact of state division

**TDP's Campaign:**
- "Unified Andhra Pradesh" sentiment
- Strong opposition to bifurcation (initially)
- Later accepted bifurcation reality
- Promised to rebuild "residual AP"

---

### 4.2 Water and Irrigation (38% of voters)

**Issues:**
- River water sharing with Telangana
- Krishna River allocation disputes
- Godavari River quota
- Irrigation project disruptions
- Agricultural water security

---

### 4.3 Unemployment (30% of voters)

**Situation:**
- Post-bifurcation job uncertainty
- Government jobs reduced
- Private sector affected
- Youth unemployment high

---

### 4.4 Regional Identity (40% of voters)

**Sentiment:**
- Andhra regionalism strong
- Rayalaseema identity distinct
- Coastal AP identity
- Post-bifurcation unity question

---

## PART 5: PARTY PROFILES

### 5.1 Telugu Desam Party (TDP) - WINNER

**Background:**
- Founded: March 1982
- Founder: N.T. Rama Rao (NT)
- Previous Rule: 1982-1989, 1992-1996
- 2014 Comeback: Under N. Chandrababu Naidu

**2014 Victory:**
- Won 102 out of 175 seats (58.3%)
- Vote share: 43.5%
- Chief Minister: N. Chandrababu Naidu (3rd term)
- Alliance: NDA (with BJP)

**Key Promises (2014):**
1. Rebuild post-bifurcation AP
2. Make Amaravati new capital
3. Industrial zones development
4. IT sector expansion
5. Water security for farmers

---

### 5.2 Indian National Congress - OPPOSITION

**2014 Performance:**
- Won 26 seats (down from higher numbers)
- Vote share: 16.2%
- Weakened position
- Lost power after Y.S. Rajasekhara Reddy's death

**Reasons for Decline:**
1. Loss of strong leader (Y.S. Rajasekhar Reddy died 2009)
2. Regional fragmentation
3. TDP's better organized campaign
4. Congress's national performance (2014 was bad year)

---

### 5.3 YSRCP - New Entrant

**First Election:**
- Only founded in 2011
- Won just 1 seat in 2014
- Got 13.6% vote share (significant for new party)
- But conversion to seats was poor
- This would change dramatically in 2019

## PART 6: BIFURCATION IMPACT ANALYSIS

### 6.1 Electoral Reorganization

**Pre-Bifurcation (undivided AP):**
- Total Assembly Seats: 294
- Total Lok Sabha Seats: 42
- Total Voters: ~67 Million
- Combined population: ~84 Million

**Post-Bifurcation (2014 residual AP):**
- Total Assembly Seats: 175 (reduced)
- Total Lok Sabha Seats: 42 (but AP's share: ~25-26)
- Total Voters: ~42.8 Million (reduced)
- Population: ~49 Million

**Result:**
- Nearly 40% reduction in electoral size
- New constituency boundaries
- New voting patterns
- Different political dynamics

### 6.2 Capital Question (Major Issue)

**Timeline:**
- June 2, 2014: Bifurcation takes effect
- Hyderabad goes to Telangana
- Visakhapatnam declared temporary capital
- 2015: Amaravati selected as new capital
- 2019: Political controversy over capital (delayed construction)
- 2024: Capital development still incomplete

## PART 8: IMPACT AND LEGACY

### 8.1 TDP Government (2014-2019)

**Achievements Promised:**
1. Amaravati New Capital
2. Industrial development
3. IT sector growth
4. Infrastructure development

**Reality (2014-2019):**
- Amaravati capital delayed
- Some industrial zones developed
- IT growth in Visakhapatnam
- Infrastructure projects ongoing
- But dissatisfaction grew

### 8.2 Road to 2019

**Events (2014-2019):**
- Water crisis in Rayalaseema
- Farmer distress
- Capital project delays
- YSRCP emerged strong (new party)
- Anti-TDP sentiment grew
- By 2019: Anti-incumbency strong

### 8.3 YSRCP's Rise (2014-2019)

**Journey:**
- 2014: Only 1 seat, 13.6% votes
- Campaign 2014-2019: Built organizational strength
- Anti-TDP narrative
- Welfare promises
- Youth appeal
- Result 2019: 151 seats (landslide)

## CONCLUSION

The 2014 Andhra Pradesh General Elections marked a historic turning point - the first election of the newly bifurcated state. TDP's victory under N. Chandrababu Naidu's leadership provided continuity and promised to rebuild the state after its division.

However, the subsequent five years (2014-2019) saw growing dissatisfaction with TDP's governance, setting the stage for YSRCP's dramatic victory in 2019. This demonstrates how post-bifurcation elections created new political dynamics in Andhra Pradesh.
